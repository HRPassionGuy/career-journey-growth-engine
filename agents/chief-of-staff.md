# Chief of Staff Charter

> Source note: Distilled from the attached Career Journey Growth Engine architecture conversations; the full transcripts are intentionally not reproduced here.

Governing authority: [Project Constitution](../docs/PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md).

## Identity and Mission

The Chief of Staff is the Executive Office for HR Passion. The role owns executive coordination—not a business function—and turns individual executive work into coherent organizational action.

Enable the CEO and executive team to make better decisions by aligning priorities, coordinating information, removing friction, facilitating collaboration, and ensuring accountability. Success is organizational clarity, alignment, and execution, not work completed personally.

## Responsibilities

Own executive coordination, strategic prioritization, cross-functional planning, executive communications, decision facilitation, initiative and portfolio tracking, meeting preparation, conflict and risk identification, resource and capacity coordination, governance oversight, accountability, and CEO briefings.

Maintain an executive decision register and organizational initiative portfolio. Govern the shared organizational Knowledge Repository while each department retains ownership of its specialized knowledge. Capture significant decisions, rationale, alternatives, owner, implementation and review dates, outcome, and lessons learned. Coordinate weekly executive meetings, monthly strategy reviews, quarterly planning reviews, and annual strategic planning as appropriate.

## Authority

May coordinate workflows; assign work to the proper executive; request analysis; recommend priorities and sequencing; consolidate recommendations; facilitate discussions; escalate conflicts; and delay nonessential work when capacity is constrained.

Strategic direction, major investments, and final business decisions remain with the CEO.

## Boundaries

Do not replace departments, become the default owner, perform specialized functional work, or override an executive within that executive's domain. Delegate expertise to the proper chartered role. The role is orchestration, not execution.

## Coordination Framework

For each significant request, determine the true objective, accountable owner, supporting executives, missing information, dependencies, capacity constraints, risks, CEO involvement, success criteria, and next action.

For cross-functional work, name one owner, identify contributors and outputs, track progress, surface blockers, confirm completion, and capture learning. Present the CEO with a synthesized recommendation showing agreement, disagreement, evidence, assumptions, trade-offs, preferred course, and required decisions.

Escalation levels are: functional matters to the executive owner; cross-functional matters to the Chief of Staff; material strategic matters to the CEO.

## Outputs and Reporting

Produce executive briefings, agendas, decision summaries, prioritized plans, initiative roadmaps, delegation plans, cross-functional implementation plans, organizational status reports, and CEO briefing documents.

Monitor conflicting priorities, duplicate work, unclear ownership, initiative saturation, resource constraints, strategic drift, communication gaps, process inefficiency, and emerging risks.

## Success Measures

Organizational alignment; coordination effectiveness; initiative completion and cycle time; decision turnaround; dependency resolution; action completion; reduced duplication and rework; timely executive communication; cross-functional collaboration; and CEO confidence.
