# Executive Operating Standard

## Executive Decision Standard

**Version 1.0**

Governing authority: [Project Constitution](../PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md).

---

# Purpose

The Executive Decision Standard establishes the organization's common framework for making, evaluating, documenting, communicating, and reviewing significant decisions.

Its purpose is to improve decision quality, ensure transparency, preserve institutional knowledge, and enable consistent executive collaboration.

This standard applies to all executives and operates in accordance with the Project Constitution.

---

# Scope

This standard applies to decisions that:

* Materially affect organizational strategy.
* Require cross-functional coordination.
* Impact customers, products, pricing, or operations.
* Allocate significant organizational resources.
* Establish or modify organizational policies.
* Create measurable organizational risk.

Routine operational decisions remain within the authority of the accountable executive, consistent with the constitutional escalation framework and the executive's charter.

---

# Guiding Principles

Executive decisions should be:

* Evidence-based.
* Mission-aligned.
* Customer-focused.
* Transparent.
* Proportionate to the level of impact.
* Documented.
* Reviewable.
* Accountable.

---

# Decision Categories

Decision authority follows the three levels established by the Project Constitution.

## Department Decisions

Department decisions are owned and resolved by the executive responsible for the functional area.

Examples include:

* Workflow improvements.
* Departmental priorities.
* Routine operational changes.

## Cross-Functional Decisions

Cross-functional decisions require collaboration between multiple executives and are coordinated by the Chief of Staff. Each decision must have one accountable executive owner, with supporting executives identified separately.

Examples include:

* Major campaigns.
* Product launches.
* Automation initiatives.
* Cross-functional process changes.

## Executive Decisions

Executive decisions are escalated to the CEO. In accordance with the Project Constitution, these include decisions involving:

* Strategic direction.
* Material investments.
* Pricing changes.
* Organizational structure.
* Significant risk.
* Mission alignment.

Constitutional amendments also require CEO direction and must follow the Constitution's requirements for intentional and documented change.

---

# Required Decision Inputs

Significant decisions should include the following inputs.

## Situation

What problem or opportunity exists?

## Evidence

What evidence supports the recommendation?

Separate:

* Facts.
* Observations.
* Assumptions.

Apply the Constitution's evidence standards, including the identification of interpretations, opinions, limitations, and uncertainty where relevant.

## Strategic Alignment

How does the recommendation support:

* Mission?
* Vision?
* Organizational goals?

## Alternatives

Identify reasonable alternatives considered and explain why they were not selected.

## Risks

Identify:

* Operational risks.
* Customer risks.
* Financial risks.
* Organizational risks.

## Expected Outcomes

Document:

* Intended benefits.
* Success measures.
* Time horizon.
* Affected stakeholders.

## Confidence

State the confidence level as high, moderate, or low, and explain major uncertainties.

---

# Decision Authority

Each recommendation should identify:

* Decision owner.
* Supporting executives.
* Required approver, where applicable.
* Implementation owner.

Only one executive may be accountable for the final recommendation. Decision authority remains subject to the Project Constitution and the applicable executive charters.

---

# Decision Record

Material decisions should record:

* Decision title.
* Date.
* Decision owner.
* Supporting executives.
* Decision category.
* Summary.
* Evidence reviewed.
* Alternatives considered.
* Risks accepted.
* Final decision.
* Decision maker.
* Success measures.
* Review date.

The Chief of Staff maintains the executive decision register in accordance with the Chief of Staff Charter.

## Initiative Handoff

Any approved decision that creates or materially changes a strategic initiative must be entered into the organizational initiative portfolio governed by the [Strategic Initiative Standard](strategic-initiative-standard.md).

The handoff must confirm the initiative's accountable executive and whether the decision's implementation owner retains implementation ownership or formally transfers it to the accountable executive. The Chief of Staff confirms the portfolio record and ownership assignment.

---

# Communication Standard

Executive recommendations should follow this structure:

1. Situation.
2. Evidence.
3. Analysis.
4. Recommendation.
5. Risks.
6. Decision Required.
7. Confidence.

This structure applies to executive reports, proposals, and cross-functional recommendations. Communication should also follow the Constitution's requirement to be clear, concise, evidence-based, action-oriented, prioritized, and transparent.

---

# Decision Review

Material decisions should be reviewed after implementation to determine:

* Was the intended outcome achieved?
* Were assumptions correct?
* Were risks managed appropriately?
* What lessons were learned?
* Should future guidance change?

Lessons learned should be contributed to the organizational Knowledge Repository in accordance with the Project Constitution. Each department retains ownership of its specialized knowledge, while the Chief of Staff governs the shared repository in accordance with the Chief of Staff Charter.

---

# Exceptions

Urgent operational decisions may proceed with incomplete information when delaying action presents greater organizational risk.

Such decisions should be documented retrospectively as soon as practical. Urgency does not change the decision authority established by the Project Constitution.

---

# Ownership

**Document Owner:** Chief of Staff

The Chief of Staff is responsible for maintaining this standard, ensuring organizational adoption, and recommending updates when improvements are identified. Updates remain subject to the Project Constitution and applicable decision authority.
