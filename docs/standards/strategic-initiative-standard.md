# Executive Operating Standard

## Strategic Initiative Standard

**Version 1.0**

Governing authority: [Project Constitution](../PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md).

---

# Purpose

The Strategic Initiative Standard establishes the organization's common framework for planning, governing, executing, monitoring, and closing significant cross-functional initiatives.

Its purpose is to ensure major initiatives are aligned with organizational strategy, have clear ownership, receive appropriate executive oversight, and produce measurable outcomes.

This standard applies to all executives and operates in accordance with the Project Constitution.

---

# Scope

This standard applies to initiatives that:

* Require collaboration between multiple executive functions.
* Significantly impact customers, products, operations, or organizational capability.
* Require executive coordination.
* Span multiple planning horizons.
* Require substantial organizational resources.
* Introduce meaningful organizational change.

Routine departmental work remains under the authority of the accountable executive, consistent with the constitutional escalation framework and the executive's charter.

---

# Guiding Principles

Strategic initiatives should:

* Align with the Mission and Vision.
* Deliver measurable organizational value.
* Have one accountable executive.
* Encourage cross-functional collaboration.
* Be evidence-informed.
* Maintain transparency.
* Adapt when evidence changes.
* Capture organizational learning upon completion.

---

# Initiative Governance

Every strategic initiative shall identify:

* Initiative name.
* Business objective.
* Strategic rationale.
* Executive sponsor, with the CEO serving where appropriate.
* Accountable executive.
* Supporting executives.
* Success criteria.
* Major risks.
* Expected completion horizon.

Only one executive may be accountable for overall delivery. Executive sponsorship does not change the decision authority, ownership, or escalation requirements established by the Project Constitution and applicable executive charters.

When an approved decision creates or materially changes a strategic initiative, the initiative must enter the organizational initiative portfolio through the handoff required by the [Executive Decision Standard](executive-decision-standard.md). The handoff must confirm the accountable executive and whether implementation ownership is retained or formally transferred. The Chief of Staff confirms the portfolio record and ownership assignment.

---

# Initiative Roles

## Executive Sponsor

The executive sponsor is an initiative designation, not a separate executive office. Within the initiative's approved direction, the sponsor provides guidance and helps remove major organizational obstacles. Significant scope changes remain subject to the approval authority established by the Project Constitution and the [Executive Decision Standard](executive-decision-standard.md).

## Accountable Executive

The accountable executive owns delivery of the initiative.

Responsibilities include:

* Planning.
* Coordination.
* Progress reporting.
* Risk management.
* Executive communication.
* Initiative completion.

## Supporting Executives

Supporting executives contribute expertise within their functional responsibilities while maintaining ownership of their respective departments.

---

# Initiative Lifecycle

Every initiative progresses through the following stages.

## 1. Opportunity Identification

Define:

* Problem or opportunity.
* Strategic alignment.
* Expected value.
* Initial evidence.

## 2. Evaluation

Assess:

* Feasibility.
* Resource requirements.
* Risks.
* Dependencies.
* Alternatives.

Material decisions concerning significant initiatives should follow the Executive Decision Standard before approval.

## 3. Planning

Define:

* Objectives.
* Deliverables.
* Milestones.
* Executive responsibilities.
* Success measures.
* Reporting cadence.
* Decision checkpoints.

## 4. Execution

During execution:

* Coordinate cross-functional work.
* Monitor progress.
* Escalate issues according to the constitutional escalation framework.
* Adjust plans when supported by evidence and approved where required.

## 5. Review

Evaluate:

* Objectives achieved.
* KPI performance.
* Budget or resource utilization, where applicable.
* Lessons learned.
* Customer impact.
* Organizational impact.

## 6. Closure

Before closing an initiative:

* Confirm success criteria.
* Complete executive review.
* Capture organizational learning.
* Transfer relevant knowledge to the shared Knowledge Repository.

---

# Initiative Reporting

Material initiatives should periodically report:

* Overall status.
* Progress against milestones.
* Risks.
* Decisions required.
* KPI performance.
* Resource concerns.
* Significant changes.
* Next major milestone.

Reporting frequency should be proportional to initiative complexity.

---

# Change Management

Material changes to an initiative should document:

* Reason for change.
* Strategic impact.
* Resource impact.
* Schedule impact.
* Risks introduced.
* Executive approval when required.

Approval follows the decision authority established by the Project Constitution and the Executive Decision Standard.

---

# Risk Management

Initiatives should actively identify:

* Strategic risks.
* Operational risks.
* Customer risks.
* Financial risks.
* Technical risks.
* Organizational dependencies.

Material risks should be communicated promptly through the constitutional escalation framework.

---

# Cross-Functional Collaboration

Supporting executives remain accountable for work performed within their departments.

The accountable executive coordinates initiative delivery but does not assume operational ownership of another executive's responsibilities. The Chief of Staff coordinates cross-functional alignment in accordance with the Project Constitution and the Chief of Staff Charter.

Cross-functional collaboration strengthens execution without reducing accountability.

---

# Success Measures

Every initiative should establish measurable outcomes before execution begins.

Measures may include:

* Customer outcomes.
* Revenue impact.
* Operational improvements.
* Community growth.
* Product adoption.
* Process efficiency.
* Quality improvements.
* Strategic capability development.

KPI design, definitions, and initiative measurement follow the Director of Analytics Charter. Functional executives retain ownership of work and experiments within their domains, with the Director of Analytics supporting measurement and evaluation.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Following completion, initiatives should document:

* What worked well.
* What should change.
* Unexpected outcomes.
* Recommendations.
* Reusable practices.

Lessons learned should improve future organizational performance and be contributed to the shared Knowledge Repository in accordance with the Project Constitution. Each department retains ownership of its specialized knowledge, while the Chief of Staff governs the shared repository in accordance with the Chief of Staff Charter.

---

# Ownership

**Document Owner:** Chief of Staff

The Chief of Staff is responsible for maintaining this standard, promoting consistent adoption, and recommending improvements based on organizational experience. Updates remain subject to the Project Constitution and applicable decision authority.
