# Executive Operating Standard

## Organizational Knowledge Standard

**Version 1.0**

Governing authority: [Project Constitution](../PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md).

---

# Purpose

The Organizational Knowledge Standard establishes the organization's common framework for capturing, organizing, governing, maintaining, and applying institutional knowledge.

Its purpose is to ensure valuable organizational knowledge is preserved, accessible, trustworthy, and continuously improves executive decision-making and organizational capability.

This standard applies to all executives and operates in accordance with the Project Constitution.

---

# Scope

This standard governs:

* Executive decisions.
* Strategic initiatives.
* Lessons learned.
* Research findings.
* KPI definitions.
* Process documentation.
* Standard operating procedures.
* Executive guidance.
* Organizational policies.
* Reference materials.

Department-specific working notes may remain within their respective functions.

---

# Guiding Principles

Organizational knowledge should be:

* Accurate.
* Current.
* Discoverable.
* Reusable.
* Evidence-based.
* Properly owned.
* Continuously improved.
* Protected from unnecessary duplication.

Knowledge is a strategic organizational asset.

# Knowledge Ownership

The **Chief of Staff** governs the shared organizational Knowledge Repository in accordance with the Chief of Staff Charter.

Responsibilities include:

* Repository structure.
* Governance standards.
* Organization.
* Lifecycle management.
* Cross-functional consistency.
* Knowledge accessibility.

Each executive remains responsible for the accuracy and completeness of knowledge produced within their functional area. Departments retain ownership of their specialized knowledge.

---

# Knowledge Lifecycle

Knowledge progresses through the following lifecycle:

1. **Creation:** The functional executive responsible for the knowledge creates the knowledge asset.
2. **Validation:** The functional executive validates the asset's accuracy and completeness.
3. **Submission:** The functional executive submits the validated asset to the shared Knowledge Repository when organizational capture is required.
4. **Repository acceptance:** The Chief of Staff confirms required documentation, ownership, and repository placement. Acceptance does not transfer responsibility for substantive accuracy from the functional executive.
5. **Publication:** The Chief of Staff publishes the accepted asset in the shared Knowledge Repository.
6. **Maintenance:** The functional executive maintains the asset's substantive accuracy and completeness. The Chief of Staff maintains its repository organization, accessibility, and lifecycle status.
7. **Review:** The functional executive reviews the asset when required by this standard. The Chief of Staff coordinates repository-level review and confirms the asset's current status.
8. **Retirement:** The functional executive recommends retirement when the asset is no longer current or useful. The Chief of Staff archives or retires it within the repository, subject to applicable decision authority.

Knowledge should remain current throughout its lifecycle.

The handoff to the shared Knowledge Repository occurs when the functional executive submits a validated knowledge asset. Repository acceptance and publication remain with the Chief of Staff, while the functional executive retains ownership of the asset's substantive accuracy and completeness.

---

# Required Knowledge Capture

The following should normally be documented:

* Material executive decisions.
* Completed strategic initiatives.
* Lessons learned.
* Approved standards.
* Research findings.
* Significant process improvements.
* KPI definitions.
* Governance updates.

Routine operational conversations need not be retained.

---

# Documentation Standards

Knowledge assets should include:

* Title.
* Purpose.
* Owner.
* Version.
* Date created.
* Date updated.
* Status.
* Related standards.
* Related executive owner.

Where practical, documents should reference related governance artifacts.

---

# Knowledge Quality

Knowledge should be:

* Factually accurate.
* Internally consistent.
* Free from unnecessary duplication.
* Clearly written.
* Properly versioned.
* Regularly reviewed.

Outdated or conflicting knowledge should be corrected or archived.

---

# Knowledge Review

Knowledge should be reviewed:

* Following major initiatives.
* Following significant decisions.
* During governance reviews.
* When standards change.
* When organizational learning identifies improvements.

Reviews should prioritize organizational usefulness over document volume.

---

# Organizational Learning

Lessons learned should identify:

* Successes.
* Challenges.
* Unexpected outcomes.
* Root causes.
* Recommended improvements.
* Reusable practices.

Organizational learning exists to strengthen future decision-making, consistent with the Project Constitution.

---

# Continuous Improvement

The Knowledge Repository should continuously evolve to:

* Improve discoverability.
* Reduce duplication.
* Increase organizational learning.
* Strengthen executive collaboration.
* Preserve institutional memory.
* Support evidence-based leadership.

---

# Ownership

**Document Owner:** Chief of Staff

The Chief of Staff is responsible for governing the shared Knowledge Repository, maintaining this standard, and recommending improvements to organizational knowledge management. Updates remain subject to the Project Constitution and applicable decision authority.
