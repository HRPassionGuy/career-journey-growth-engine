# Executive Operating Standard

## Executive Communication Standard

**Version 1.0**

Governing authority: [Project Constitution](../PROJECT_CONSTITUTION.md).

---

# Purpose

The Executive Communication Standard establishes the organization's common framework for executive communication, reporting, recommendations, and escalations.

Its purpose is to improve clarity, consistency, decision quality, and cross-functional collaboration by ensuring all executives communicate using a common structure and shared expectations.

This standard applies to all executives and operates in accordance with the Project Constitution.

---

# Scope

This standard applies to executive communications including:

* Strategic recommendations.
* Decision requests.
* Initiative updates.
* Performance reports.
* Risk escalations.
* Cross-functional coordination.
* Executive briefings.
* Status reports.

Routine operational conversations do not require this level of structure.

---

# Guiding Principles

Executive communication should be:

* Clear.
* Concise.
* Evidence-based.
* Action-oriented.
* Transparent.
* Audience-appropriate.
* Timely.
* Focused on decision-making.

Communication should reduce uncertainty rather than create it.

---

# Standard Communication Structure

Material executive communications should follow this structure.

## 1. Situation

Describe:

* Current situation.
* Problem or opportunity.
* Relevant background.
* Why attention is required.

## 2. Evidence

Present:

* Facts.
* Data.
* Observations.
* Research.
* Customer insights.

Clearly distinguish evidence from assumptions or opinions in accordance with the Constitution's evidence standards.

## 3. Analysis

Explain:

* Interpretation of the evidence.
* Strategic implications.
* Risks.
* Trade-offs.
* Alternatives considered.

Analysis should demonstrate reasoning rather than simply restate evidence.

## 4. Recommendation

Provide:

* Proposed action.
* Expected benefits.
* Supporting rationale.
* Expected organizational impact.

Recommendations should be specific and actionable.

## 5. Risks

Identify:

* Operational risks.
* Customer risks.
* Financial risks.
* Strategic risks.
* Dependencies.
* Mitigation strategies.

Material risks should be escalated according to the constitutional escalation framework.

## 6. Decision Required

Clearly identify:

* Whether approval is required.
* Required decision maker.
* Desired decision date.
* Implementation implications.

If no decision is required, state that the communication is informational.

## 7. Confidence

State confidence as:

* High.
* Moderate.
* Low.

Explain significant uncertainties, assumptions, or information gaps.

# Executive Reporting

Periodic executive reports should include:

* Current priorities.
* Progress against objectives.
* KPI performance.
* Significant accomplishments.
* Risks.
* Decisions required.
* Upcoming milestones.

Reports should emphasize meaningful change rather than activity.

---

# Cross-Functional Communication

When collaborating across departments:

* Clearly identify the accountable owner.
* Identify supporting executives.
* Communicate dependencies.
* Confirm expected deliverables.
* Record material decisions.

Cross-functional communication should improve coordination without creating duplicate ownership. Cross-functional matters are coordinated by the Chief of Staff in accordance with the constitutional escalation framework. Each initiative retains one accountable executive, as established by the [Strategic Initiative Standard](strategic-initiative-standard.md).

---

# Escalation Communication

Escalations should include:

* Nature of the issue.
* Organizational impact.
* Urgency.
* Evidence.
* Risks of inaction.
* Recommended response.
* Required decision.

Escalations should follow the constitutional escalation framework: department matters are resolved by the responsible functional executive, cross-functional matters are coordinated by the Chief of Staff, and executive-level matters are escalated to the CEO.

---

# Communication Quality Standards

Executive communication should avoid:

* Unsupported conclusions.
* Ambiguous ownership.
* Unclear recommendations.
* Missing evidence.
* Excessive detail.
* Unnecessary urgency.
* Hidden assumptions.

Every communication should help the organization make better decisions.

---

# Documentation

Material executive communications that result in organizational decisions should be preserved in accordance with:

* The [Executive Decision Standard](executive-decision-standard.md).
* The [Strategic Initiative Standard](strategic-initiative-standard.md).
* The Organizational Knowledge Standard.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Executives should periodically evaluate whether communication:

* Improved decision quality.
* Reduced ambiguity.
* Supported collaboration.
* Improved organizational learning.
* Increased execution effectiveness.

Recommendations for improving executive communication should be submitted to the Chief of Staff.

---

# Ownership

**Document Owner:** Chief of Staff

The Chief of Staff is responsible for maintaining this standard, promoting consistent executive communication practices, and recommending updates as organizational needs evolve. Updates remain subject to the Project Constitution and applicable decision authority.

---

# Relationship to Other Standards

This standard complements—not replaces:

* The [Executive Decision Standard](executive-decision-standard.md).
* The [Strategic Initiative Standard](strategic-initiative-standard.md).
* The KPI & Performance Standard.
* The Executive Operating Rhythm.
* The Organizational Knowledge Standard.

Its role is to standardize how executives communicate, while those standards govern what they communicate and when they communicate.
