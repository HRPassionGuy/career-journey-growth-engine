## Script 1 — More applications will not fix unclear positioning

**Estimated delivery:** 75–90 seconds  
**On-screen hook:** `100+ applications and no interviews? Audit the message before increasing the volume.`

**Final spoken copy**

If you have submitted more than a hundred applications and the response is silence, your next move may not be another hundred applications.

It may be a positioning audit.

Start with four questions.

First: can a recruiter name your target role within a few seconds, or does your résumé try to qualify you for everything?

Second: does the top third show proof? A list of words like “strategic,” “innovative,” and “results-driven” is not proof. Give the reader context for the problems you solve.

Third: do your bullets stop at responsibilities, or do they show specific outcomes? You do not need to invent numbers. Explain what changed because of your work—fewer delays, a clearer process, a better employee experience, a stronger decision.

Fourth: does the document sound like you? AI can help you compare a job description or tighten a sentence, but generic language makes qualified people sound interchangeable.

The goal is not to beat a system with more keywords. The goal is to help a human understand where you fit and what evidence supports it.

Before you increase your application volume, audit the target role, top-third proof, outcomes, and human voice.

**Recording and quality notes**

- Show the four audit labels on screen: `TARGET ROLE`, `TOP-THIRD PROOF`, `OUTCOMES`, `HUMAN VOICE`.
- Keep the tone diagnostic and respectful; do not blame the job seeker.
- Do not claim that this audit guarantees an interview.

## Script 2 — AI literacy in HR is more than writing prompts

**Estimated delivery:** 75–90 seconds  
**On-screen hook:** `Want to show AI literacy in HR? Demonstrate judgment, not just tool use.`

**Final spoken copy**

If you want to demonstrate AI literacy as an HR professional, saying “I use AI” is not enough.

Employers need to understand how you use it—and where your human judgment takes over.

Use this four-part framework.

One: name the business problem. Were you organizing employee feedback, preparing interview questions, reviewing a process, or drafting a first version of a communication?

Two: explain what AI helped you do. Maybe it helped you identify themes, generate alternatives, or rehearse scenarios.

Three: show the judgment you retained. What did you verify? What bias, privacy, policy, or context risk did you check? What did you reject or rewrite?

Four: explain the human outcome. Did the final work make a decision clearer, a conversation more respectful, or a process easier to use?

That is the difference between tool familiarity and responsible capability.

In HR, the durable skills still matter: listening, coaching, culture awareness, evidence-based decisions, and trust. AI can support the work. It should not replace accountability for the work.

So when you describe AI literacy, do not lead with the tool. Lead with the problem, the assistance, the judgment, and the human outcome.

**Recording and quality notes**

- Show the framework on screen: `PROBLEM`, `AI ASSISTANCE`, `HUMAN JUDGMENT`, `HUMAN OUTCOME`.
- Avoid naming a specific AI product so the lesson remains evergreen.
- Do not imply that AI literacy alone guarantees advancement or employment.
