## Script 1 — Your résumé may be making the recruiter do the work

**Estimated delivery:** 75–90 seconds  
**Suggested on-screen hook:** `Qualified—but still not getting interviews? Run this 3-part résumé diagnostic.`

**Final spoken copy**

If you’re qualified, applying consistently, and still not getting enough interviews, stop asking, “What’s wrong with me?” Ask a better question: “What does my résumé make easy for the recruiter to understand?”

As an HR leader, I can tell you that a résumé can contain strong experience and still hide the reason someone should call you.

Run this three-part diagnostic.

First: can I identify your target role in the first few seconds, or does your résumé sound like it could belong to five different careers?

Second: does the top third show proof, or is it filled with adjectives like “strategic,” “results-driven,” and “passionate” without context?

Third: do your bullets explain outcomes? “Managed onboarding” names a responsibility. “Redesigned onboarding to remove delays and improve the new-hire experience” shows judgment and impact. Use numbers when they are accurate, but never invent them.

Your goal is not to stuff in more keywords. Your goal is to make your fit clear, specific, and human.

If you want a structured way to audit your positioning, résumé, interview preparation, and next-career move, go to the link in my bio and select the YOU COACHING SERIES.

**Recording notes**

- Deliver the opening as a calm pattern interrupt, not a fear-based warning.
- Pause after each diagnostic question.
- Put `TARGET ROLE`, `TOP-THIRD PROOF`, and `SPECIFIC OUTCOMES` on screen as the three teaching beats.
- Do not add a job guarantee, interview guarantee, salary promise, discount, or new offer language.

## Script 2 — Your interview answer may explain the work but hide your judgment

**Estimated delivery:** 75–90 seconds  
**Suggested on-screen hook:** `If your interview answers sound like job descriptions, use this diagnostic.`

**Final spoken copy**

Here’s one reason a capable professional can leave an interview feeling good and still not move forward: the answer explained the work, but it never revealed the judgment behind the work.

Listen for the difference.

“I handled employee relations cases” tells me your responsibility.

But a stronger answer helps me understand how you think: What was the situation? What made it difficult? What options did you consider? What action did you personally take? And what changed—or what did you learn?

Before your next interview, choose three stories and run this five-part diagnostic:

One: is the problem clear?

Two: is your role separate from the team’s role?

Three: can I hear the decision you made?

Four: is the outcome specific and truthful?

Five: can you explain what you would repeat or improve?

That last question matters because employers are not only listening for a polished success story. They are listening for self-awareness, judgment, and the ability to grow.

You do not need a perfect career story. You need relevant evidence, clearly explained.

If you want a structured way to prepare your career story, interview examples, and next move, go to the link in my bio and select the YOU COACHING SERIES.

**Recording notes**

- Use a conversational HR-leader tone; avoid sounding like a memorized interview formula.
- Show the five diagnostic questions as brief on-screen text.
- Emphasize “specific and truthful.”
- Do not add a job guarantee, interview guarantee, salary promise, discount, or new offer language.
